Inside an Electric Car: How Touchscreens, AI, Chips and Software Work

A modern electric vehicle may look like a computer on wheels: a wide touchscreen shows navigation, battery percentage, energy consumption, climate controls, cameras and music. But where does that information come from? Is the display itself an AI computer? What processor, RAM, operating system and vehicle network make the interface work—and how can carmakers update it safely?

Quick answer: Most EV digital cockpits combine a display and touch controller with an automotive system-on-chip (SoC), memory, storage, graphics software and protected connections to other vehicle controllers. The battery-management system (BMS), other electronic control units (ECUs) and sensors provide vehicle data. AI is used for some features, such as voice interaction or camera analysis, but the battery-percentage display and ordinary touchscreen menus do not inherently need generative AI.

1. Instrument cluster versus touchscreen versus passenger screen

A digital instrument cluster sits in front of the driver, traditionally replacing analogue speed and warning gauges. It may show speed, gear or drive mode, battery state of charge, estimated remaining range and essential warnings. The central infotainment touchscreen is usually used for navigation, media, climate settings, charging options and other vehicle functions. Some vehicles add a passenger display or a head-up display (HUD).

Three physical screens do not necessarily mean three independent computers. A single automotive SoC can drive multiple outputs, or a vehicle can use multiple processors or isolated software domains. The passenger screen is not standard equipment on every EV, and entertainment restrictions depend on how the vehicle is designed and operated.

These cockpit ideas also appear in modern petrol and hybrid cars. EV-specific details include battery management, charging and regenerative-braking information, not the touchscreen concept itself.

2. What happens when a finger touches the screen?

Many automotive touch displays use projected-capacitive touch sensing. A touch controller detects changes in electrical capacitance near its sensor grid and reports coordinates to the software. A display controller and graphics pipeline then draw the interface based on those inputs. The underlying screen may use an LCD or OLED panel, with vehicle-grade brightness, temperature and reliability requirements.

  1. The user taps a control, such as climate temperature.
  2. A touch sensor and controller identify the location of the interaction.
  3. The infotainment operating system delivers a touch event to the relevant application.
  4. The application requests an authorised change through the car's interface layer.
  5. A vehicle controller acts on the request if allowed, and the interface receives a status update.

Touchscreens are not meant to replace the safety decisions of the brake, steering or traction controllers. Vehicle architecture places limits on which apps can read or command vehicle functions. Some controls also remain physical for usability and safety.

4. What operating system runs inside a car touchscreen?

A car display is not automatically running the same Android experience as a phone. Automotive software is a collection of operating systems, middleware, protected vehicle interfaces, graphics tools and application frameworks. Different vehicles use different combinations.

Google explicitly separates Android Auto and Android Automotive OS. Its automotive platform documentation also explains optional Google Automotive Services. QNX Cabin documentation describes configurations combining infotainment guest operating systems with safety-oriented QNX software.

Do not assume a specific EV runs AAOS or QNX solely because it has a large screen; use the automaker's documentation for that model and software version.

5. How do the battery and sensors communicate with the screen?

An EV contains multiple electronic control units (ECUs). Some supervise battery health and power, some process vehicle speed and body functions, and others handle braking, steering, cameras or connectivity. These units exchange information over in-vehicle networks, including CAN bus, LIN and Automotive Ethernet, depending on the system.

The Battery Management System (BMS) monitors relevant battery measurements and estimates state of charge (SoC) and health. The central screen usually displays values received from authorised vehicle services rather than directly reading individual battery cells.

For Android Automotive, Google's Vehicle Hardware Abstraction Layer (VHAL) exposes defined vehicle-property interfaces and metadata. It is a software abstraction, not a claim that all vehicles use a particular wire or bus. Permissions and platform components determine which data an app may access. Android's automotive architecture documents this distinction.

Safety principle: A media app should not gain unrestricted access to critical vehicle commands just because it can display battery information. Gateways, access controls and software-domain isolation matter.

6. How does the screen show battery percentage and remaining range?

The state of charge (SoC) is the battery's estimated remaining charge expressed as a percentage of its usable reference. The BMS estimates it using measurements and models; it is not simply a fuel-tank float sensor. A dashboard range prediction additionally considers usable energy, recent or expected energy use, driving conditions and manufacturer-specific calibration.

A simple teaching formula is:

Remaining energy (kWh) ≈ Usable pack energy (kWh) × SoC fraction
Estimated range (km) ≈ Remaining energy (kWh) ÷ Consumption (kWh / 100 km) × 100

Suppose a hypothetical EV has 60 kWh usable battery energy, 75% SoC and an assumed driving consumption of 15 kWh per 100 km. Then remaining energy is 45 kWh and:

Range ≈ (60 × 0.75) ÷ 15 × 100 = 300 km

Important limitations: Nominal and usable battery capacity may differ. Battery temperature, aging, charging reserves, cabin heating or air conditioning, speed, traffic, gradients, tyres and energy-recovery behaviour can change actual range. Vehicle algorithms may incorporate historical driving, navigation and battery protection constraints. The example is not a certified range claim.

Units to remember: kW describes instantaneous power; kWh describes energy. A 60 kWh battery is an energy capacity, while a 60 kW charger is a power rating. Real charging speed varies with battery temperature, state of charge and charging equipment.

7. How does the screen display charging and regenerative braking?

During charging, the battery management and charging subsystems communicate information such as battery temperature, available charging current, voltage and state of charge. The car may show charging power in kW, delivered energy in kWh and an estimated completion time.

Regenerative braking uses the electric drive system under appropriate conditions to recover some kinetic energy and return it to the battery. The display may visualise energy flow or recovered power. It does not create free energy, and not every braking event results in identical regeneration because battery, traction and safety limits apply.

A conceptual dashboard calculation for charging time might use remaining energy ÷ effective average charging power, but this is a rough estimate. A real charging curve is not constant; it can taper significantly as SoC increases.

8. Which cockpit features actually use AI?

Not every digital feature is AI. A responsive map, touchscreen slider or battery bar can be implemented with traditional software. Machine learning becomes relevant where the application needs patterns, speech, vision or adaptive predictions.

Voice assistantSpeech recognition, intent processing, sometimes generative AISome voice features use ML; not all use large language models
Navigation and EV trip planningRouting algorithms, maps, charging database, consumption forecastsNo, rules and optimisation can work without ML
Battery range forecastBMS physics, statistical models, historic driving dataNo, algorithm choice is manufacturer-specific
Camera and driver monitoringComputer vision and embedded ML where equippedOften ML-based in modern systems, but hardware varies
ADAS alertsCameras, radar, perception, safety control softwareMay use ML, but not equivalent to autonomous driving
Climate touchscreen buttonUI event, permission, vehicle controller commandNo, straightforward control logic works

These features may use different processors from the infotainment touchscreen. An ADAS camera processor might run a safety-oriented perception workload in its own domain. A dashboard's visual animation does not indicate where those computations run. Automakers must manage failures, driver distraction and safety boundaries.

Mahindra's XEV 9e and BE 6 demonstrate why automotive chips matter in Indian electric vehicles. Mahindra announced the vehicles in November 2024 and described the XEV 9e's wide cockpit with three screens measuring approximately 31.24 cm each, while the BE 6 uses a different digital-cockpit presentation. Mahindra's launch announcement.

Qualcomm's official January 2025 announcement identifies Snapdragon Digital Chassis / Cockpit Platform technology in the BE 6 and XEV 9e, along with its automotive connectivity technology. Qualcomm release. Mahindra's follow-up described Snapdragon 8295, 24 GB RAM and 128 GB storage in its published cockpit platform specification.

Do not generalise those figures: hardware and feature availability depend on vehicle generation, market, trim and software release. These are cited manufacturer claims about a product, not a teardown of every vehicle. A proprietary “AI-powered” marketing label alone does not tell us which task uses on-device AI or the exact model in use.

10. How do over-the-air (OTA) updates work, and why does cybersecurity matter?

Software-defined vehicle features can be updated after a car leaves the factory. Depending on the vehicle, an update may change an infotainment app, system firmware, maps or a specific ECU. A secure update process should verify authorised sources and package integrity, apply the right compatibility and installation rules, handle failures safely and keep a trustworthy record of versions.

Attack surfaces may include mobile-app integrations, Bluetooth/Wi-Fi, cloud-connected features, physical service ports, software supply chains and OTA delivery. Defences can include authenticated updates, secure boot, network segmentation, least-privilege application access, monitoring and incident response.

The US NHTSA vehicle-cybersecurity guidance discusses risk reduction throughout the vehicle lifecycle. The UK's Vehicle Certification Agency explains UN Regulations R155 (cybersecurity) and R156 (software updates) within applicable vehicle approval frameworks. Requirements differ by market, vehicle category and model year.

Safety and security caution: This is an educational explanation—not a guide to bypass vehicle gateways, connect to safety-critical CAN networks or modify firmware. Diagnostics, calibration and update installation on a real car should follow the manufacturer's authorised procedures.

11. Usability, driver distraction and personal data

More screens are not automatically better. Safety-relevant alerts must remain understandable; complex menus should not distract a driver. The permitted behaviour of a passenger display may differ from the central touchscreen. Voice interactions can reduce some physical interactions but can still cause cognitive distraction.

Connected vehicles can process location history, media preferences, account information, call data, telemetry or camera-related data, depending on features and consent. Users should examine the vehicle's privacy settings and the automaker's documentation rather than assuming all data stays locally or that every feature constantly uploads it.

12. Can developers build EV touchscreen applications?

Developers can learn with Android Automotive emulators, published automotive app frameworks and UI prototypes. A small educational project might display simulated speed, battery percentage and charging status without connecting to a physical vehicle. Real vehicle properties and sensitive controls may be gated by operating-system permissions, certification and manufacturer APIs.

Google provides Android for Cars developer guidance, including supported app categories, driver-distraction considerations and testing. A safe beginner workflow is to prototype with mock values first, review permissions, and never depend on undocumented vehicle control APIs for a public app.

13. Final takeaway

The display is the visible surface of a larger embedded computing system. Underneath it, processors and memory run the interface, vehicle ECUs provide protected data, networks connect control systems, and software turns information into useful screens. AI supports selected voice, vision and prediction functions; it does not replace the hardware, safety engineering or traditional software logic that make the cockpit work.

This is why EV digital cockpits are an interesting meeting point for embedded Linux/Android systems, semiconductors, networking, automotive engineering, AI and cybersecurity.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EV touchscreen itself an AI computer?

It is a display and input device attached to an embedded computing system. The computer may include an NPU or AI software, but many ordinary dashboard features do not require AI.

Is Android Auto the same as Android Automotive OS?

No. Android Auto projects supported phone apps to the car's display. Android Automotive OS is a car-integrated platform installed on the vehicle's computing hardware.

Does the battery percentage come from the touchscreen?

No. The BMS estimates battery status from measurements and models, and the cockpit displays authorised information provided by vehicle software interfaces.

Can any infotainment app control the car's brakes or motor?

No. Well-designed vehicles restrict access to safety-critical functions through permissions, hardware/software isolation and approved control paths.

Does every Mahindra EV have 24 GB RAM?

No. The cited Mahindra statement describes its announced cockpit platform; hardware and equipment vary by model, trim and revision. Always check the specific vehicle's documentation.

How is remaining range calculated?

A simplified calculation is remaining usable energy divided by expected energy consumption per kilometre. Real cars also factor in conditions, limits and manufacturer-specific algorithms.
